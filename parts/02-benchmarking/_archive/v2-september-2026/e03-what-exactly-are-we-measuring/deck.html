bluff-v1 · construct

Homodyne

Behavioural calibration under a stated penalty: answer when P(correct) > t, say “I don't know” otherwise — and move the line when t changes.

Neighbour 1

Knowledge

Accuracy. Reported by Bluff, as a diagnostic only — it cannot see bluffing.

Neighbour 2

Stated calibration

Confidence that matches correctness. GPT-4: calibrated after pre-training, less so after post-training. Not measured here.

Neighbour 3

Faithfulness

Staying inside a given document — Vectara's number. Bluff gives no document. Not measured here.

spec.yaml · line one

Homodyne

The decision this number must change.

decision.question
Which of the tested models can sit behind a short-answer QA feature in which a confident wrong answer costs more than “I don't know” — in English and in Persian?
cost_of_being_wrong
A user acts on a confident fabricated answer. At t = 0.75, one wrong answer costs three times what a right one is worth. Set by the product, not the model.
decision_horizon
Until the next major release of any tested model; at most six months.

spec.yaml → claim_boundary

Homodyne

Written before there is a result to defend.

Supports

Model X's penalised score at t = 0.75 on these 650 questions, with its interval — above or below zero.

Whether abstention rose as the stated penalty rose.

An English–Persian gap, only if larger than the declared detectable size.

Who breached the fabrication guardrail on unanswerable items.

Does not support

“More honest”, “smarter”, “better”.

Less hallucination in long answers, summaries or RAG.

Anything about consumer chat apps or the next release.

A rank where intervals overlap.

The answer

Homodyne

We measure hallucination. Too vague to test

We measure whether a model answers only when it should — when told what a wrong answer costs.

And we wrote down what that misses.
