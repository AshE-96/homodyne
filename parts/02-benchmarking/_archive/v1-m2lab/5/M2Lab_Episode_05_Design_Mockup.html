Accuracy would read beautifully here. Call the other eight non-competitors correctly and it climbs — even though the two competitors that mattered were missed.

In discovery, recall wins: don’t lose a real rival. In a board report, precision wins. If a miss costs ten times a false alarm, F1’s equal weighting is a choice you made by accident.

The mean can hide the tail

Tool A

mean 7.8 · median 8.4 · worst decile 1.6

1score per case10

Excellent most of the time, and then four cases where it collapses.

Tool B

mean 7.6 · median 7.8 · worst decile 4.2

1score per case10

Slightly lower on average, and it never falls off a cliff.

Two tenths of a point separate the means. For a product, how a tool loses matters more than how much it wins by. A leaderboard shows neither the tail nor the worst group.

Rank is not a confidence interval

78.4Tool A
77.8Tool B
+0.6observed difference
−1.9 +3.1

The interval covers zero. That does not mean the two tools are equal — it means the current data and method do not show a clear advantage.

A difference can be statistically real and far too small to switch for. With a 48-case pilot the interval will be wide — and that width is the finding, not something to hide.
