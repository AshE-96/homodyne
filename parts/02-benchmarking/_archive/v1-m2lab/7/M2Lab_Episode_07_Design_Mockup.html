When the score becomes the target

01The test distribution is known, fixed and narrow.

02Many variants can be tried without disclosure.

03Only the best result becomes public.

04Benchmark data is reachable for training or tuning.

05The incentive to rank outgrows the intended use.

Optimising against a benchmark is not cheating. If the benchmark really represents the work, getting better at it is the point. The risk starts when the distribution is narrow and the claim stays enormous.

We do not guess at anybody’s intent. We examine the structure.

Four states an audit is allowed to return

Clear

Public evidence is sufficient and points the same way.

Limited

Evidence exists but is partial, or rests on an important assumption.

Concern

There is specific evidence of a risk that affects interpretation.

Unknown

No public evidence found. Not a zero, and not a failure.

Unknown is not safe. Unknown is not failed.
It means we have not yet earned a conclusion.

When the evidence disagrees with itself

Independent study

Raises concerns about variant testing and disclosure

  • Private variants can be tested before a public entry
  • Selective disclosure of which results appear
  • Asymmetric access to interaction data

Platform response

Disputes parts of the reading, and publishes more method

  • The scores in question sit inside overlapping intervals
  • The testing policy was already public
  • Ranking method, rank spread and the ranking code were opened
Conflicting

Both sources stay open. An audit that forces a verdict here would be inventing one — and the platform’s response and subsequent method changes are part of the evidence, not a footnote to it.
