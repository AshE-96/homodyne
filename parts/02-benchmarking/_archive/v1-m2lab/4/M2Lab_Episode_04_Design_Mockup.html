Difficulty needs a reason

Easy

Clear brief, direct sources, a known market, facts that hold still.

What it exposesNothing yet — this is the floor everything else is measured against.

Medium

Several sources must be combined, a term is ambiguous, market definitions differ.

What it exposesWhether the tool synthesises or just quotes the first result.

Hard

Thin data, conflicting sources, a blurred market boundary, recent information.

What it exposesWhether it says “unknown”, or invents something plausible.

Edge

Rare, but the failure is expensive: a fabricated competitor, an invented citation, the wrong currency, two companies sharing a name.

What it exposesThe failures you cannot ship past, at any score.

Define hard as “whatever today’s models fail at” and the taxonomy collapses as models improve. Every case needs a written failure hypothesis. “It’s hard” means it isn’t designed yet.

Public data can become training data

Snapshot takenEvery fact stamped with a source and a date
Dataset frozenAnswer key held back, holdout kept private
Main runTools executed under the fixed protocol
PublishedFrom here the cases can leak into future training
Contamination The benchmark, or its pattern, was probably seen during training. Suspected, rarely proven.
Leakage An answer key reached the prompt, or a reviewer saw the tool’s name. This one is inside your own run.
“Contamination-free” Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. Report the risk and what you did about it — never the phrase.

Ground truth here is a reference process, not an answer key

01 Verifiable factsFact, source, snapshot date, confidence level
02 Claim supportSupported · partial · unsupported · contradicted
03 Rubric dimensionsActionability and uncertainty, with behavioural anchors
04 Pairwise judgmentBlind choice between two reports, for one named decision

They don’t replace each other. Facts see correctness, the rubric sees usefulness, pairwise sees the fine difference. Collapse all four into one rating and you can audit none of them.

Twenty of the twenty-eight disagreements sit in one pair of cells. The annotators are not unreliable in general — they are unreliable about exactly one boundary.

So the fix is not retraining people. It is rewriting the definition of Partial, versioning the annotation guide, and re-checking the labels that were made under the old one.

High agreement is not validity — annotators can agree on a wrong label. And low agreement is sometimes the benchmark telling you the question itself is badly designed.
