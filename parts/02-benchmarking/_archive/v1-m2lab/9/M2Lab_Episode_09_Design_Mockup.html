Five layers, and a reader question for each

Result“What happened?” Profile, primary estimate, uncertainty, guardrail status, date
Method“How did you measure?” Specification, dataset card, protocol, metric registry, analysis plan
Evidence“Where did this number come from?” Case, run, claim, label, source, raw and derived output
Decision“What does it mean for me?” Scenario recommendation, caveats, insufficient-evidence zones
Governance“What happens next?” Versions, changes, issues, conflicts, corrections, rerun schedule

Publish only the first layer and you have shipped a claim with a number attached to it.

Never published

  • Keys, tokens, credentials
  • Personal or private data
  • Copyrighted full text beyond licence
  • Sensitive prompts or exploits
  • Holdout answers that would break the benchmark

Published instead

  • Derived labels
  • Permitted excerpts
  • Hash and timestamp
  • A locator
  • The reason it is withheld

“Not public” must never mean “not documented”.

Say how far reproducibility actually goes

Reproducible

Code, data and environment let somebody recompute the result.

Realistic for open models and static datasets.

Partially reproducible

Part of the pipeline is open; the product or the web underneath is closed or moving.

Usually the honest label for a mixed setup.

Auditable only

The evidence and the method can be inspected, but the system cannot be re-run as it was.

The honest label for live consumer products.

Claiming full reproducibility when nobody can obtain the previous version of a closed product is simply false. Picking the weaker label is not a weaker result — it is an accurate one.

Minor Compatible cases or metrics are added.
Major The claim, the distribution or the scoring changes. Before and after are no longer directly comparable — and the page has to say so.

Rerun quarterly: 80% anchor cases held fixed, 20% fresh, plus an off-cycle run after a major release. A trend line is drawn only where method and dataset are compatible.
