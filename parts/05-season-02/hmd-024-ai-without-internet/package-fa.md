**English description (dub / site):**

```
Does AI really work without the internet? We switched off the Wi-Fi and gave 30 fixed Persian and English tasks to an AI running on this laptop, next to an online service.

Device: MacBook Air M1, 8 GB. Runtime: Ollama (free, open source). Three small models: Qwen3.5 2B and 4B (1.9 and 3.3 GB) and Gemma 3 4B (3.4 GB), all quantized to Q4_K_M. Tasks: summarise in three points, extract dates, amounts and names to JSON, answer from a passage (two questions have no answer in it), translate, classify tone or topic, and one category that is hard on purpose: multi-step reasoning and a very long text. All texts are synthetic and were locked before any run. Grading uses an answer key; translations are graded blind by the host; no AI grades another AI. Before the local run, the program tried a few well-known internet addresses, and all had to stay silent; again during and after. The online service is a much larger model with different settings; every arm had the same 1,024-token answer cap.

Result: [RUN — one line from the report].

Product decision: tasks the local model handles stay offline, the rest go to a stronger model, and confidential text never goes to the cloud by default.

Claim boundary: one laptop, three small models, 30 synthetic tasks, one Ollama version, one date; not a verdict on all local AI. Offline alone is not security. Online timing includes the network. Energy and depreciation are stated assumptions, not measurements. The 15 → 4.6 GB example is Llama 3.1 8B from the llama.cpp docs, not our models.
```
